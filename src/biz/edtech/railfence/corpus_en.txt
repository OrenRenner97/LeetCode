Every morning the old ferry leaves the harbour a little after six, when the water is still grey and the gulls are arguing over whatever the fishermen threw away the night before. Most of the passengers are the same people every day: workers going to the shipyard on the other side, a few students with heavy bags, and a woman who sells bread at the market and always carries more loaves than she can possibly sell. Nobody talks much at that hour. They stand by the rail, watch the town grow smaller, and think about the work that is waiting for them.

The captain has been doing this crossing for almost thirty years. He knows every rock under the surface and every change in the wind, and he says that the sea is never the same twice, even when it looks exactly like yesterday. When the weather turns bad he does not cancel the trip unless the harbour master orders it. People trust him, and that trust is the reason the small company has survived while larger ones have closed.
